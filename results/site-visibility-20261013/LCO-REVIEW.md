# Independent review of the LCO archival follow-up

**The bounded LCO result is supported: no inspected frame supplies a predicted transit measurement, and the two authorized baseline frames fail ordinary core-photometry feasibility for TIC 80387959 and TIC 80387965 because of saturation. Neither conclusion changes either candidate's planetary status.** No blocking scientific issue remains for this interpretation.

The reviewer inspected the frozen image allowlist, retained timing and catalog records, registration implementation, FITS data from exactly the two authorized images, and the corrected figure. No additional stellar flux, target spectrum, network download or reserved epoch was opened. [review_lco.py](review_lco.py) preserves independent arithmetic and pixel checks; [LCO-verification.json](LCO-verification.json) records results and hashes of both image files.

## Verified evidence

- **Timing:** all 55 retained metadata entries miss the propagated frozen event envelope. Independently recomputed period/epoch centers, all five profile-pair bounds, heuristic floors and exposure-overlap decisions agree. The nine science frames precede the nominal midpoint by approximately 3.84–3.18 hours. For the two downloaded exposures, an additional full topocentric calculation changes the geocentric BJD by +19.88 and +20.12 milliseconds, far too little to change the no-overlap result. The original timing envelope remains heuristic; this result is not an in-event non-detection.
- **Saturation:** an independent whole-image circular mask, rather than the producer's cropped indexing, reproduces all reported saturation, nonlinearity and bad-mask counts for all five plotted sources in both frames. In both frames, the header saturation threshold is 128,800 and the nonlinearity threshold is 122,360. The primary has 104/111 saturated aperture pixels across the two frames; TIC 80387965 has 20/18. These are substantial failures of the declared ordinary-core extraction criterion. They do not prove that every possible specialized saturated-star method would fail.
- **Registration:** the corrected figure visibly aligns the target and neighbors with their expected source configuration. The affine fit uses dozens of consensus matches and gives small withheld-match residuals. However, translation and match selection use all stars before the split; these residuals are diagnostics, not blind independent astrometric validation. Nearby unsaturated catalog entries many arcseconds from the saturated stars cannot supply their widths. The report explicitly retains the failed naive-WCS identification and disclaims those widths.
- **TIC 402358350:** the two representative science headers name HD 182572 and point 5.29318° and 5.29346° from the candidate. Their 0.0275°/pixel WCS implies 57.2° across 2,080 pixels, substantiating the warning that footprint matches do not identify a target spectrum. No candidate radial velocity or absence of variability can be inferred.

## Corrections and remaining limits

The review flagged a minor coordinate-epoch distinction: the catalog's approximately 13.8″ target/neighbor separation is not the exact separation of the positions drawn in these registered images. The drawn combination of Gaia J2016 primary and retained TIC neighbor coordinates gives approximately 13.03″ (33.47 pixels). The author was asked to label the different epochs rather than report the earlier catalog separation as a new image measurement. This does not affect the saturation finding; both source cores are plainly distinct and separately exceed the threshold.

The metadata API's inconsistent count/next fields and one repeated ID prohibit a claim of exhaustive archive coverage; the author's assessment preserves this limitation and the explicit pagination history. Neither two baseline images nor successful registration establishes transit-depth precision, a resolved event, or a source attribution. Shorter exposures and future conditions need independent feasibility and sensitivity checks. The original completed investigations remain complete; decisive planetary or nonplanetary evidence remains unavailable from this bounded test.
