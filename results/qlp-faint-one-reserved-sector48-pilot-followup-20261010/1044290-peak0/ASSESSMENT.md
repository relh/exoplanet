The screening-eligible signal refines to period 5.968604254976 days, epoch BTJD 2612.772736224902, duration 0.133056 days. Three supported events give local depth 6,694 ± 670 ppm. The native discovery plot was visually inspected: a noisy localized dip and positive shoulders appear amid substantial time-series gaps. This does not locate the source.

Reserved Sector 21 measures -669 ± 763 ppm at the frozen phase. The conditional timing spread is 1.738148 days, larger than the event duration, so this is timing-inconclusive rather than confirmation or precise rejection. Alternate processing, pixel and catalog checks are recorded below. No credible planet, host attribution or novelty claim is made.

At the frozen ephemeris, same-epoch QLP gives 6,694 ± 670 ppm, while SPOC gives -710 ± 412 ppm. Both use the same images; the processing disagreement is not an independent temporal test.

Both raw and processed difference images were visually inspected. Three event windows show a strong compact feature far from the target. The processed moment is 4.165 pixels from the catalog target and 0.354 pixels from 1044282; the latter lies at the processed stamp edge, so its moment can be truncated. The 0.0368-pixel distance to the brightest-image moment is NOT a target offset. Both raw spatial-background fits (67 and 39 background pixels) preserve the off-target compact peak. Their plots were visually inspected. Formal pixel SNRs near 670/663 are not calibrated host probabilities.

Neighbor TIC 1044282's QLP Sector 48 aperture measures 270,406 ± 546 ppm at the frozen primary phase, and 259 ± 545 ppm at half phase. This deep same-image eclipse, combined with localization, strongly favors neighboring-source contamination. It does not independently establish the orbital period, exclude an unresolved companion to the neighbor, or validate recurrence. Sector 21 gives -225 ± 632 ppm at the fixed phase, with the target's timing prediction already inconclusive.

No credible planet candidate on TIC 1044290 is promoted. The leading explanation is flux contamination from the neighboring eclipsing source; the precise physical system and fundamental period remain unconfirmed.
