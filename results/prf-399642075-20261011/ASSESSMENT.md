# TIC 399642075: dimming favors the known eclipsing-neighbor region

Both discovery-sector stacks (37–38) favor TIC 905950916, whose Gaia DR3 identity is 5231593599046170112, over TIC 399642075. Gaia classifies that neighbor as ECL and lists a frequency consistent with the VSX 2.2882-day period; this closely matches the TESS 2.28867-day feature. All 21 leave-one-event-out stacks favor the same neighbor. This strengthens the contamination explanation and the decision to deprioritize an on-target planet interpretation.

Source attribution within that neighborhood is less secure: TIC 905950922 is a close competing source. Among 18 registration perturbations at −0.1, 0, +0.1 pixels on each axis, four favor that competitor and fourteen favor TIC 905950916. Individual-event preferences vary as well. The original bright target is not favored by these perturbed stacks. Target-minus-best chi-square differences are 341 and 318, while the two close neighbors differ by only 18 and 26. Formal diagonal errors and PRF mismatch make these numbers conditional diagnostics, not probabilities.

The catalog eclipse depth also leaves a quantitative dilution caveat: Gaia's modeled primary depth is about 0.107 magnitude, whereas a roughly 206-ppm dip in the bright target's light would require a substantially larger fractional loss for a T=17.29 neighbor under equal-aperture-throughput assumptions. Bandpass, catalog blending and aperture differences prevent a direct equivalence, but we have not demonstrated that this cataloged neighbor supplies the entire TESS amplitude. The close brighter neighbor remains relevant. Do not equate a matching period and preferred image fit with a fully solved physical source.

Scene registration uses a T<=18 catalog scene with relative fluxes fixed from Tmag, a common displacement, normalization and background plane, with linear TIC proper motions. The initial free-flux scene fit had too many parameters for the valid pixels and failed; that attempt is preserved. Matched-model synthetic registration recovers a known offset, but excludes real PRF/calibration errors. The source comparison is posthoc and no epoch is an independent discovery here.

Raw pixel/catalog/mission-model hashes are checked; all 1,079 stacked, individual-event and omission fits reconstruct independently with a QR solver. Three Gaia DR2-to-DR3 mappings and all CSV/JSON records reconcile, including the eclipsing-variable identity. Plots were inspected. Sector 65 remains unopened at this assessment. No new planet is established.
