# TIC 402358350: external archive and contaminating-source follow-up

The new evidence does **not** establish or exclude the source of the 11.77-day TESS signal. It identifies the published dominant period of the already-known pulsating neighbor and opens independent ZTF photometry of a different close neighbor. Both fixed-ephemeris tests remain inconclusive. No target disposition change is supported.

## Most useful findings

**TIC 402358249 is a published SPB candidate pulsator.** The exact Gaia DR3 identifier, `4318716236117290496`, occurs in the SPB table of [Aerts, Molenberghs & De Ridder (2023), A&A 672, A183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07870), with dominant frequency **0.9517724/day**, period **1.050671358 days**, and G-band amplitude **0.0072604 ± 0.0008012 mag**. The exact row is retained in [pulsator_paper_spb.json](pulsator_paper_spb.json), with catalog schema and [ReadMe provenance](paper-readme-source.json). This adds the published period to the prior Gaia variability flag. It is not a new variable-star discovery. There is no established correspondence to the 11.77-day TESS dips.

The exact-source Gaia epoch series contains 46 rows, of which 43 pass the frozen G-band quality criteria. Its normalized scatter is 0.83%, versus median formal uncertainty 0.108%. The predeclared descriptive periodogram's highest peak, 1.050642805 days, recovers the known variability to the grid precision. This uses the same underlying Gaia observations as the published period, so is a consistency check, not independent confirmation. Multiple strong aliases remain. We neither optimized the TESS clock nor inferred a planet from this periodogram.

**TIC 1918682563 has four ZTF streams positionally consistent with its Gaia counterpart**, `4318715480203031936`. Each cloud position is within one arcsecond of that source. These are a different source from the pulsator above. The four frozen OIDs yield 389 raw and 288 quality-passing measurements, across g and r. Relative flux scatter is very large compared with the roughly 2% formal errors; the per-stream 5th–95th percentile ranges span approximately 0.47–1.69 of median flux. This could reflect real variability, blending or measurement systematics. Positional association does not prove uncontaminated photometry, and these data do not by themselves establish an eclipsing binary.

## Fixed-clock results and their limits

The separate [Gaia plan](epoch-plan.json) and [ZTF plan](ztf-plan.json) were saved before their respective flux accesses. Both preserve four clocks: original discovery refinement with 1.5/2.5-day detrending windows and expanded pre-sector 81 clocks with the same two windows. Those refinements and timing profiles are copied into the plans with immutable source hashes. External-series inspection is a retrospective follow-up of selected TESS signals, not a new blind validation exercise. The “original” here is the saved discovery-refined clock, not the earliest coarse screening peak.

For Gaia, G-band TCB times were converted to TDB BTJD. Only finite positive flux/error rows passing both supplied rejection flags were used. Inside points lie within half the saved duration; baseline points lie beyond 1.5 durations. The propagated timing uncertainty is approximately 2.54–2.59 days for original refinements and 0.249–0.411 days for expanded clocks, versus half-durations 0.09072/0.072 days. There are zero or one predicted in-event samples. All four tests therefore fail the timing or event-count gates. One original-clock sample is bright; one expanded-clock sample produces a formal 2.56σ dip. Neither supports exclusion or detection. [Full measurements](epoch-results.json), [plot](gaia-diagnostic.png).

For ZTF, all four preselected streams were retained and normalized separately; combined g and r fits retain separate OID intercepts. Combined-filter duplicate observations within one second would retain the lowest OID deterministically; none were removed from the quality-passing set. No g+r fit or period search was made. HMJD was converted by undoing the heliocentric correction at Palomar and applying a barycentric TDB correction, under both UTC and TDB interpretations of the input scale. The scale difference and one-second allowance enter the timing gate. Every one of the 48 clock/scale/stream combinations is inconclusive.

For the expanded primary clock under the UTC interpretation:

| Stream | Quality samples | In-window samples | Fitted relative dip | Formal error | Error scaled to observed residual scatter |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Combined g |82|3 in 2 cycles|−15.5%|1.43%|20.1%|
| Combined r |206|1 in 1 cycle|−31.6%|2.27%|29.9%|

Negative dips mean the sampled points are brighter than the fitted baseline. The formal significance is misleading because a constant per-stream baseline is a poor description of these widely scattered measurements. Expanded-clock timing spread alone is 0.145–0.152 days, already exceeding the 0.072-day half-duration. Hence these fits neither exclude a contaminant eclipse nor show one at the TESS clock. The 1-second duplicate rule groups samples within each filter only; same-night samples with different timestamps remain separate observations, while event-cycle counts prevent treating them as independent transit events. [All 48 measurements](ztf-results.json), [plot](ztf-diagnostic.png).

As an illustration only, the cached TIC T-band flux ratio gives a neighbor flux fraction 0.00266365 under equal aperture throughput and a two-star scene. Producing a 635-ppm total-flux dip would then require a 23.84% intrinsic neighbor dip. Formal combined g/r sensitivity to such a dip would be 16.7/10.5σ **if the photometry were a constant baseline with the quoted errors and the timing were accurate**. Those assumptions fail here; scatter-scaled sensitivity is only about 1.2/0.8σ, the profiles are imprecise, and actual TESS throughput and optical-band eclipse depths are unknown. These figures are not exclusion limits.

## Archive searches and identity safeguards

- Previous target [novelty](../novelty-402358350-20261011/ASSESSMENT.md) and [external coverage](../external-coverage-402358350-20261011/ASSESSMENT.md) checks were reviewed first. Exact-name web searches for HD 353367, TYC 1602-2473-1 and 2MASS J19403616+1538250 yielded no useful new primary source. Irrelevant numerical/name coincidences were discarded. This is not proof of novelty.
- Exact SIMBAD neighbor querying recovered the pulsator's bibliographic entry, leading to the published SPB table above. The direct Gaia `vari_ms_oscillator` query returned zero rows; that absence did not prevent recovery of the published frequency from VizieR. A first bibliography query used the wrong join column and failed HTTP 400; its error response is retained, alongside a successful corrected query. See [query provenance](simbad_neighbors.json), [failed query](pulsator_references.json), [recovery](pulsator_references_recovery.json).
- Public VizieR metadata queries within 60 arcsec of the target found no entries in APOGEE DR17 (`III/286/catalog`), LAMOST DR5 (`V/164/dr5`), RAVE DR6 (`III/283/ravedr6`) or the queried WDS table. These constrain only those releases and services. WDS coordinate semantics were checked using a separate name-prefix query. No spectra were opened. ESO queries were deliberately left to the parent investigator to avoid duplicate work.
- The ZTF selection reused the saved October 11 metadata cone and froze all four unambiguous OIDs before cloud access. The DR24 cloud record counts differ from older metadata counts; the retrieved records and filters, not a post hoc preferred curve, determine the analysis. TIC 1918682448's multiple nearby Gaia associations remain ambiguous and were excluded from this experiment. TIC 1918682446 likewise has multiple Gaia mappings; neither has been silently assigned a unique identity.
- The target Gaia parallax and the two investigated neighbors' parallaxes differ substantially; simple proximity does not demonstrate a bound companion. TIC 1918682563 has RUWE 2.93, which cautions against treating its astrometry as a complete physical model. We did not infer an orbit or stellar radius from it.

## Reproducibility and stopping point

All external raw responses and cloud JSON exports are in ignored `data/web-402358350-20261012/`, with URLs/queries/IDs and SHA-256 hashes in the adjacent JSON manifests. Cloud JSON is an exported representation of selected rows, not a byte-for-byte Parquet archive. The saved Gaia and ZTF plans precede access; each frozen plan remains separate from result files. Initial missing `lsdb` and failed SIMBAD query routes are recorded; cloud retrieval subsequently completed all four OIDs. No pending archive process remains.

`verify.py` passed 94 independent checks of Gaia identity, quality selection, two-part-JD time conversion, timing-profile arithmetic, QR depth/error estimates, periodogram sinusoid fits and response hashes. `verify_ztf.py` passed 397 checks of frozen source hashes and chronology, all selected OIDs, raw exports, position associations, independent time iteration, deterministic exposure selection, timing propagation, event counts, QR fits and scatter-scaled errors. Both plots were inspected: Gaia shows percent-level variability with multiple periodogram aliases; ZTF shows broad scatter across time and phase rather than a clean fixed-clock dip. [Gaia verification](verification.json), [ZTF verification](ztf-verification.json), [dependency provenance](environment.json).

The numerical experiments are bounded and complete. No TESS epoch was downloaded, no shared scientific code changed, and no external submission was made. The missing discriminant remains precise, spatially attributable photometry of the 11.77-day events. Repeating this sparse fixed-fold exercise or selecting a favorable alias would not resolve the host. The catalog `Teff` field for the published pulsator is 3.9657 despite a Kelvin label in the supplied schema/ReadMe; it is preserved but intentionally not used to infer temperature or planet properties.
