# Campaign evidence review

Across the two disjoint audited tiers, 34,255 distinct selected stars have
34,177 usable searches after recovery and 78 remaining unusable targets.
These are dated, scoped selection counts. They are not a complete census of
TESS, a count of planet-free stars, or a calibrated sensitivity result.
Recovery in alternate sectors is explicitly distinguished from repair of the
original failed searches.

Current earlier-tier follow-up coverage is 107 unique peaks across 95 stars.
The exploratory one-reserved tier contributes 25 follow-up peaks, not necessarily
25 distinct stars. The refreshed earlier audit retains duplicate records but
counts them once, finds no missing required artifacts, and finds no unaccounted
peak under the retrospective diagnostic gates. Assessment hashes reflect the
latest raw-image and quality reviews. Existence and provenance do not validate
a scientific interpretation.

The investigation has materially extended the search, but the desired likely
new-planet outcome remains unproven. Do not declare a discovery or settle source
attribution on the basis of these accounting checks.

The strongest recurring retained signal, TIC80387959 at4.19217days, appears in
multiple TESS epochs. Its processed/raw/PRF host diagnostics conflict; correlated
TGLC extractions and sparse NSC/SkyMapper photometry do not remove nearby-source
alternatives. A useful next research step must test that ambiguity rather than
add another copy of a periodogram. The available raw pixels and retained
per-event maps permit examining whether the inconsistent host preference is
driven by particular event windows or common-mode residuals; any such test
must retain the original ephemeris and model limitations.
