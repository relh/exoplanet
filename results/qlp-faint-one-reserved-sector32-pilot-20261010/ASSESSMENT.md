# Sector 32 exploratory search: no qualifying signal

All 230 selected targets produced usable searches: 207 below threshold, 23 flagged,
zero errors. Neither the original follow-up policy nor the two-event diagnostic
policy admitted a peak. Terminal inventories and source hashes reconcile.

[Visual inspection of two flagged signals](../qlp-faint-one-reserved-sector32-flagged-review-20261010/ASSESSMENT.md)
supports retaining the flags: TIC 1357792 has broad oscillatory variability;
TIC 372953580 has deep alternating eclipselike events and an odd/even mismatch.
No planet or physical-host claim is made for either. Reserved sectors remain
unopened because no signal qualified.

Controls used the first three frozen targets (595087, 1353822, 1357792):
12 short and 12 longer trials, all in their actual search grids. Exact baseline
array agreement was verified for both detrending windows.
Short trials: six period/phase recoveries and five passes.
Long trials: eight period/phase recoveries and four passes.
All nine screening passes have valid phase recovery. One is an Earth-radius
trial on TIC 595087 at 0.83 d; the longer passes are 5.73 d at two/three
Earth radii on TIC 595087 and 1353822. Original stellar variability is retained.
This small selected control sample does not estimate completeness or exclude
planets among the unqualified targets.

Metadata-only inventory excluding the complete selection leaves 1907 unused
stars under the one-distant-reserved-sector rule. Sector 30 is selected next
with 201 untouched targets; no corresponding photometry was opened.
This is a scoped archived pool, not exhaustive all-TESS coverage.
