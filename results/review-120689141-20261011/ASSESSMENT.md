# TIC 120689141: period matches published stellar rotation

**Updated identity and literature check:** SIMBAD links TIC 120689141, HD 177780 and KIC 5774694 to the same object. Beck et al. (2017), [Table 1](https://pure.au.dk/ws/files/134029931/Lithium_abundance_and_rotation_of_seismic_solar_analogues.pdf), lists a surface rotation period of 12.1 ± 1.0 days, attributed to García et al. (2014). Our 12.153-day peak agrees with that published rotation period. Together with the broad, processing-dependent shape, this makes rotational variability a strong explanation and removes the signal from new-planet priority. This is not proof that all dips are rotational or that the star lacks planets. The original García PDF request returned HTTP403; the retrieved Beck table is the evidence used here. Raw hashes and exact aliases are retained.

Original discovery assessment, retained for context:

Sectors 40–41 produce a passing 12.153066-day, 263-ppm box signal with formal SNR 15.12 and duration 0.18 day. Four events have local SNRs 4.06–5.94. Noise inflation reduces the heuristic SNR to 8.37. The conditional on-target size would be 1.77 Earth radii; no planet radius is established.

The inspected fold shows a broad depression extending roughly ten hours on either side of the fitted event. It is substantially wider than the 4.32-hour search box. The alternative detrending window retains the period but increases depth to 591 ppm. A sinusoid explains 0.898 times the box likelihood improvement. These are substantial variability/processing concerns despite the automated pass.

VSX returns one nearby rotational variable, KIC 5689517, at approximately 56.3 arcseconds using the catalog's rounded coordinates. Its catalog period is 10.469 days. This is a neighbor, not an exact identity match to the target, and the period differs. Its contribution to the aperture is untested. Catalog non-matches at the exact target position do not establish novelty.

Sector 81 remains reserved and unopened. Later-epoch recovery and pixel-level source attribution are outstanding. Kepler coverage and the target's exact catalog identity merit checking given the field location. This signal remains unresolved and is not a secure planet candidate. The frozen discovery record, query URL and verified raw catalog hash accompany this assessment.
