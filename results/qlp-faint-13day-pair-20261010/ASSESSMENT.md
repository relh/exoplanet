# Selected 13-day pair: shared-event hypothesis not established

This post-hoc comparison uses the original QLP training only and both previously
frozen ephemerides. TIC 198186473 and TIC 233194992 are 8.05205 degrees apart.
Their predicted event centers differ by 0.0713–0.1132 days over eight training
cycles, exceeding the sum of their transit half-durations (0.0600 days).
Their nominal transit boxes therefore do not overlap.

At its own ephemeris, TIC 198186473 measures 12,053 ± 894 ppm. At the other
star's ephemeris it measures −1,861 ± 754 ppm. TIC 233194992 measures
7,483 ± 834 ppm at its own ephemeris and −1,496 ± 981 ppm at TIC 198186473's.
Neither fixed cross-measurement recovers a positive dip. These are selected,
dependent comparisons and are not calibrated hypothesis probabilities.

Both are on Camera 4. TIC 198186473 is on CCD 4/3/3/3 in Sectors 83–86;
TIC 233194992 is on CCD 3/3/2/2. Detector headers and raw hashes are retained.
The pair does not demonstrate a common identical event or local aperture blend.
A broader camera or processing systematic with differing timing remains possible,
but these measurements neither identify nor exclude such a cause.

The similarity of periods alone must not be presented as proof of contamination
or a shared artifact. Both leads remain unpromoted on their individual evidence:
TIC 198186473 has a matched-exposure QLP–SPOC discrepancy; TIC 233194992 fails
alternative detrending and lacks SPOC files. Reserved timing remains inconclusive.
No new observing data, fitted ephemeris, or candidate claim was introduced.
