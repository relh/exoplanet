# TOI-1435 b: additional discovery data strengthen a weak seed, but holdout stays inconclusive

Adding all four previously unopened early sectors (22, 41, 48, 49) to the original pair (14, 15) strengthens a local fit around the original strongest peak. The catalog orbital period is not supplied to that fit. The refined six-sector period is 0.685814792 days and its box depth is 172 ppm, with formal SNR 10.69. Alternate detrending gives 0.685817296 days, 184 ppm and SNR 10.92. Both are close to the known planet's catalog period; this remains a known-planet control, not a discovery.

A matched original-only local refinement gives SNR 6.04 at the first window and 6.37 at the alternate window, versus 10.69 and 10.92 after adding data. Thus the improvement is not merely the finer local-refinement grid. The original global discovery search had SNR 5.96. This is a local search seeded by that original strongest peak, not a new full-domain search or a pass through all original vetting gates. Other periods, correlated noise and source attribution are not fully tested by this experiment.

The expanded clock and timing profile were saved and verified before opening reserved sector 76. At that unchanged clock, the reserved data give 112 ± 47 ppm (formal SNR 2.41). The largest measurement among the 12 previously saved timing pairs is SNR 2.86. Expected formal SNR for the discovery depth is only 3.70. The timing spread is 0.04663 day, greater than half the fitted 0.048-day duration, so the existing follow-up rule classifies this as timing-inconclusive. It supplies no strong independent recovery and is not evidence that the known planet is absent.

The six discovery products, both local refinements and their fixed-clock measurements reproduce from hashed data. The four added downloads postdate the plan. The independent-sector verifier checks the unchanged clock, freeze-before-download chronology, raw identity/hash and all 12 profile measurements. The comparison plot was inspected. Old control audits asserting sector-76 cache absence describe the earlier pre-follow-up stage and are not expected to pass after this deliberate opening.

This single selected control suggests a useful way to revisit some below-threshold seeds using additional data, while exposing the limits of one reserved sector. It does not establish a recovery rate, statistical significance calibration or new planet candidates. The original control outcome remains a miss under its original search design.
