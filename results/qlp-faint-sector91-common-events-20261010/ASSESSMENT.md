# Sector91: shared timing undermines the planet interpretation

Ten eligible period solutions belong to five distinct stars, including the pilot
TIC386739254. Every solution uses two supported QLP events; all second event
centers lie within 42.96 minutes near BTJD3796.36, and their fitted transit windows
overlap. Stars are separated by 2.52–13.49 degrees. Three solutions also share a
first event near BTJD3787.37 (347752079 peak0, 98718764 peak1, 110976002 peak1).
See [timing plot](timing.png) and [hashed inputs and comparison](comparison.json).

This post-selection comparison strongly favors shared instrumental contamination
for these period solutions. Aliases on a star are not independent trials; no
false-alarm probability is assigned. Background-sensitive difference images show
negative, broad, target-centered and neighboring-source residuals depending on
fit and star. A target-centered residual alone therefore does not establish a
planet. All earlier-sector predictions remain timing-inconclusive. No candidate
is promoted, and the exact instrumental mechanism remains unresolved.

[NASA's Sector91 notes](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/tess/sector91_summary.html)
document loss of guiding, unstable pointing, and Earth/Moon scattered light.
These are contextual evidence, not an identification of the particular cause at
BTJD3796.36. The fetched page's URL/hash are retained. No masks or search gates
were changed based on this comparison, and it does not exclude other planets.
