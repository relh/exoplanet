## Newer QLP searches and missing uncertainties

The 1,000-target metadata request timed out. Four 250-target requests subsequently
completed: 11,184 observations, including 1,267 observations of 445 stars beyond
Sector 85. The failure and successful requests are recorded separately.

A new 60-star selection prioritized both a low measured noise floor and legacy
maximum BLS S/N below 8, motivated by the injection audit's missed signals amid
existing variability. Forty-three were searched using archive uncertainties;
17 initially lacked usable uncertainty values in at least one selected sector.

The missing-error inputs contained usable quality-zero photometry. We explicitly
retried them with a documented fallback: where **no** usable archive errors exist,
assign equal errors from the robust MAD of adjacent flux differences, divided by
sqrt(2). Pairs crossing gaps are excluded. Sectors with usable published errors
retain them. The assumption, scale, pair counts, original flux product and source
hash are recorded per sector. It estimates short-timescale scatter, not calibrated
correlated-noise significance. The existing detrending, binned-noise and other
checks still apply. A deterministic test checks noise recovery with gaps and dips.

All 17 retries completed, as did analogous retries of the previous 16 missing-error
inputs. Thus **113 distinct legacy targets also received newer QLP searches**:
53 from the earlier selection and 60 from the new one. Eighty used published
uncertainties throughout; 33 required an explicit empirical-error fallback in at
least one sector. None passed all non-crowding cuts. These are overlapping stars,
not 113 extra unique targets, and no complete sector survey is implied.

### TIC 301798878: preserve the ambiguity

This is HIP 12642 / LP 410-81, classified M1.5V by the retrieved
[SIMBAD record](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2012642).
TIC 471011362 at the same position is explicitly a duplicate of 301798878, not an
independently resolved neighbor. Catalog snapshots did not match either TIC in
the queried confirmed/TOI files; this is not a novelty or exhaustive literature check.

The initial formal S/N is 8.53, with three supported events. QLP supports the
covered events in Sectors 42 and 44; Sector 43 has no usable event measurement.
The exposure model gives a duration about 0.0335 days and depth about 1,354 ppm,
but this is not a physical transit fit or radius measurement.

The automatic Sector 44 difference moment lands 4.34 pixels away because another
image residual is stronger. **That is not sufficient to reject this signal as
off-target.** Separate event images show positive residuals in the target core,
roughly 880 and 961 ppm after a simple background subtraction. The external
residual lies outside the SPOC aperture. Localization remains ambiguous and
uncalibrated; retain the actual images rather than trusting the global maximum.

The initial prediction for Sector 70 has about 0.272 days of timing spread, far
larger than the transit duration. Its low fixed-phase S/N is inconclusive. We
explicitly reassigned Sector 70 to training, wrote that plan, fitted a new
training ephemeris, then froze it **before** opening still-unused Sector 71.
The revised period is 19.61819 days. Sector 71 has no usable event measurement,
and extrapolated timing spread is still about 0.111 days. There is no successful
independent prediction here.

Block-permutation controls exceed the original BLS maximum in 3/16, 2/16 and
0/16 draws for 0.5-, 1- and 2-day blocks: **5/48 total**. This conditional
diagnostic preserves only limited correlation structure and is not a survey
false-alarm probability, but it weakens an already marginal peak. Keep this as
an unresolved lead for possible future data, not a likely planet candidate.

### TIC 290653184: coherence is not enough

No one of 48 block controls exceeded its original maximum. That does not make
it a planet: coherent stellar or instrumental signals can also pass this test.
The roughly day-wide feature and factor-two depth change with detrending remain
problematic, as do its pixels and QLP disagreement.

Adding older Sectors 8/35 to training refined a 9.77667-day ephemeris before
opening Sectors 61/72. Sector 61's fixed depth S/N is 1.84 despite expected formal
S/N 10.91 and a conditional timing bound below half the fitted duration. Sector
72 has total S/N 6.18 but fewer than two supported events, so it does not meet the
recovery criterion. These formal, box-model diagnostics are not physical validation
or proof that the star has no planets. All results are retained.
