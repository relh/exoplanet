# Small-star multi-sector campaign — 2026-10-09

**No likely new planet candidate is established. The broader search goal remains
active.** This batch substantially expands the pilot, but the two signals passing
its initial cuts do not yet survive the stronger evidence checks.

## Selection and coverage

We inventoried all 85 ordinary sector target lists linked by the fetched
[TESS-SPOC archive page](https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/tess-spoc): 4,038,466 distinct
TIC IDs. These lists identify target-pixel coverage; a few listed stars have no
light-curve product. All 85 list downloads succeeded. This does not cover all
TESS products: [QLP](https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/qlp) has newer sectors as well.

A complete MAST TIC query returned 60,026 stars with radius 0.15–0.8 solar,
T magnitude 6–12 and distance <=120 pc. Additional cuts removed known TIC matches
in the TOI, confirmed-planet, CTOI and MAST v1.0 eclipsing-binary snapshots;
TIC duplicate/artifact dispositions; contamination ratio >0.2 or missing; and
Teff >5300 K or missing. We required >=2 adjacent sectors <=55 for discovery and
>=1 sector >=56 reserved for validation. **11,851 stars were eligible.**

The first 300 were ranked by radius² × 10^(0.2 × (Tmag−10)) / sqrt(training sectors).
This is a sensitivity proxy, not measured photometric quietness. The earliest
qualifying group, limited to three sectors, was searched. The selected training
sectors span 50 distinct sectors between 1 and 55. Older full-frame combinations
are a reasonable search opportunity because the archive's documented multi-sector
FFI searches start with the later 200-second observations; this does not establish
that these stars or combinations are unsearched in the literature.

All selected TIC IDs, stellar properties, sector lists, exclusions, ranking rule,
query parameters, retrieval timestamps and source hashes are retained in
`selection-small-stars-20261009/`. Catalog non-matches do not prove novelty.

## Discovery search

The search concatenated independently detrended training sectors and tested
0.5–20 day periods, bounded by baseline/2.5. Durations were 0.04, 0.06, 0.09,
0.13 and 0.18 days. We retained three period-separated likelihood peaks, applying
additional diagnostics above formal S/N 7. Existing screening flags include a
stricter S/N 8 threshold, odd/even and secondary tests, individual event support,
sinusoidal/inverted controls, crowding and an alternate detrending window.

**299 of 300 targets were searched successfully.** TIC 176954932's Sector 1
light-curve URL returned HTTP 404 despite its presence in the target list. That
error is retained, not counted as a null detection. The campaign has finished;
all target results are in `campaign-small-stars-20261009/targets/`.

Two initial filter passes were inspected:

| TIC | Discovery period | Depth | Formal S/N | Further evidence |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 394015919 | 12.0390 d | 251 ppm | 10.47 | Does not reproduce at the refined ephemeris in QLP Sectors 27/28: formal depth S/N −2.66 and 1.22. |
| 467361658 | 13.5381 d | 226 ppm | 13.26 | Highly inconsistent event depths; QLP supports a dip in Sector 18 (S/N 5.87), but not 17 or 19 (0.64, 0.25). |

For TIC 467361658, six local event depths give chi-square 132.05 for 5 degrees of
freedom under the formal independent-error model. This is a useful inconsistency
diagnostic, not a red-noise-calibrated probability or a physical classification.
Some deep excursions are close to observing-segment boundaries. A background or
instrumental explanation requires pixel/neighbor work before any candidate claim.

The QLP tests used a frozen ephemeris refined on TESS-SPOC training data. They
compare extractions of the same images, not independent observations. Differences
in aperture or processing can either create or suppress a signal; disagreement
therefore blocks promotion here but does not prove no planet exists.

## Held-out observations

We wrote `frozen.json` before opening any held-out light curve for each inspected
repeating lead. Fixed-period/epoch measurements were made without adjusting the
ephemeris to those later observations. The timing profile and an epoch-error
floor were propagated to the prediction dates. These bounds assume a box model
and formal white errors; they are not physical or red-noise-calibrated posteriors.

The three inspected repeating leads (including the weaker TIC 52373734) all have
large extrapolated timing ranges: several days. Consequently all seven inspected
held-out sectors are **timing-inconclusive**, regardless of the fixed-phase S/N.
It would be incorrect to call these successful predictions or decisive failures.
Their files are in `followup-*-20261009/`.

The initially untouched later sectors used were 71/72 for TIC 52373734, 67/68 for
TIC 394015919, and 58/59/73 for TIC 467361658. Those sectors cannot be represented
as still-blind data in subsequent work. Other older sectors and, for some targets,
newer unused sectors remain available for a better train/validation split.

## Single-event leads

TIC 311931991 (BTJD 1809.2555) and TIC 178278439 (BTJD 2576.5638) each have only
one supported deep event. Their apparent long periods are aliases allowed by gaps,
not measured orbital periods. The events appear in TESS-SPOC SAP and PDCSAP, but
differ strongly in QLP. At the former event QLP shows a brightening; at the latter,
the QLP corrected median dip is only about 339 ppm versus about 7000 ppm in the
TESS-SPOC local measurement, and disappears/reverses in other QLP apertures.
Neither is promoted as a periodic candidate. These are extraction-dependent
features requiring pixel-level investigation if pursued further.

The inspected products, download hashes, MAST observation-query snapshots, plots
and numerical comparisons are in `inspection-small-stars-20261009/`. QLP products
lack FITS checksum keywords; that absence is recorded, with HTTPS-download hashes
retained. TESS-SPOC files use direct whole-HDU checksum verification.

## Sensitivity and software validation

We selected 20 of the quietest already-completed training targets at the time of
the audit. Artificial exposure-integrated transits were injected **before**
detrending into their real photometry: period 4.37 days, duration 1.92 hours,
500 ppm and 1000 ppm depths. A recovery required a top-three period within 0.5%
and formal S/N >=7.

**19/20 were recovered at each depth: 38/40 total.** These are deterministic
probes at one period/duration/phase in a quiet subset, not survey completeness,
false-alarm calibration, or proof of sensitivity to every Earth-size planet.
The retained noise and existing astrophysical variability make them more useful
than testing only synthetic white-noise curves. See `sensitivity-small-stars-20261009/`.

Twelve tests pass, covering synthetic transit recovery, noise/variability controls,
gap handling, FITS checksums/corruption, big-endian FITS arrays, multi-sector
recovery, selection separation, fixed-phase validation and timing propagation.

## Remaining work toward the active goal

- Expand beyond the initial 300 of 11,851 eligible stars, and include newer QLP
  coverage. Use measured training noise to prioritize quiet stars.
- Add event-depth consistency and data-gap/background diagnostics, while retaining
  potential transits in variable stars for residual searches.
- Use unused observing seasons to improve training ephemerides, preserving at
  least one genuinely untouched season for a decisive prediction test.
- Calibrate detection reliability with time-correlated nulls and trial counts;
  broaden injection/recovery across periods, depths and durations.
- For any surviving signal, perform current live EB/variability and positional
  neighbor checks, pixel/centroid/aperture vetting, independent extraction, physical
  transit fitting and literature checks. No external submissions have been made.
